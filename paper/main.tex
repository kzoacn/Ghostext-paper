\title{Ghostext: Auditing Finite-Message Leakage in LLM Steganography}
\author{}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Language-model steganography often evaluates next-token probabilities, although
an observer receives a finite text with an endpoint. We show how termination
can expose content--length dependence even when the entire marginal length
distribution is matched. A terminal-information criterion yields asymptotic
token-transcript total variation one on specified finite-state Markov sources with positive
varentropy. We establish its concentration premise for three ideal mechanisms:
arithmetic-prefix recovery, published rotation range coding, and its later
reverse-verified midpoint rule, with nonzero finite certificates on an explicit
dependent source. A dependent zero-varentropy source gives exact
equality instead; fixed exogenous endpoints have an explicit delivery-failure
tradeoff. Separately, executing the original Neural Linguistic Steganography
encoder on an exhaustive finite-input example gives terminal TV $15/16$ while
reported KL is zero. A guarded-prefix repair bounds Ghostext's integer-coding
error against a stopping-matched reference, while leaving a public endpoint
predicate. We execute unchanged current rotation range-coding code on 160
instances each for GPT-2 and Qwen2.5-0.5B. Scoring every published string gives
verified-rule held-out AUCs $0.923/0.971$ and $0.966/0.975$ at 128/512 bits;
independent-endpoint AUCs range from $0.481$ to $0.549$.
Doubling independent token budgets raises ID recovery from 74/160 to 158/160
and from 89/160 to 154/160, with text recovery reaching 129/160 and 133/160.
Processed-source studies and authenticated-text endpoint
replays provide separate implementation evidence. The results distinguish
sampling accuracy, reliable recovery, and security of complete transmitted texts.
\end{abstract}
