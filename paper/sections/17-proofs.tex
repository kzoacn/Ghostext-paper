\section{Proof Details for Termination Results}
\label{app:proofs}

\subsection{An Explicit Markov Bound}
\label{app:markov-proof}

For a positive binary transition matrix $P$, condition on the even-state
skeleton $Y_0,Y_2,\ldots,Y_{2m}$, where $m=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$. If $n$ is odd,
also condition on $Y_n$. The remaining odd bridge states are independent.
A bridge with endpoints $(a,b)$ chooses its middle state zero with probability
\[
 \theta_{ab}=\frac{P_{a0}P_{0b}}{P_{a0}P_{0b}+P_{a1}P_{1b}},
\]
and its two possible information contributions differ by
$d_{ab}=|\log_2(P_{a0}P_{0b}/(P_{a1}P_{1b}))|$. The last edge when $n$ is odd is
constant under this conditioning. At least $m/4$ bridges have one common
endpoint pair. Condition on all other odd states; the remaining information
is a shifted, possibly reflected, scaled binomial variable with at least
$m/4$ trials. The selected bridge type may depend on the skeleton, since the
bound below is uniform over the four types.

For $K_s\sim\operatorname{Bin}(s,\theta)$, Fourier inversion gives
\begin{align*}
 \sup_j\Pr(K_s=j)&\le\frac1{2\pi}
 \int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|1-\theta+\theta e^{it}|^s\,dt\\
 &\le\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2\sqrt{2s\theta(1-\theta)}}.
\end{align*}
For the second inequality, use
$|1-\theta+\theta e^{it}|^2=1-4\theta(1-\theta)\sin^2(t/2)$,
$\sin(|t|/2)\ge|t|/\pi$, and $1-u\le e^{-u}$, then extend the resulting
Gaussian integral to the real line. A band of width $w$ contains at most
$\lfloor w/d_{ab}\rfloor+1$ lattice values. Removing the conditioning proves
Eq.~\eqref{eq:explicit-markov} with
\[
 d=\min_{a,b}d_{ab},\qquad
 K=\max_{a,b}\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2\sqrt{2\theta_{ab}(1-\theta_{ab})}}.
\]
For Eq.~\eqref{eq:dependent-source}, the four $\theta$ values are
$9/11,3/5,3/5,1/3$, and the four gaps are
$\log_2(9/2),\log_2(3/2),\log_2(3/2),1$. These give the stated $d$ and $K$.
The conditional information variance is at least
$m(6/25)[\log_2(3/2)]^2$. Total variance and the finite-state variance limit
therefore give $\sigma^2\ge(3/25)[\log_2(3/2)]^2>0$.
With $a=\log_2(4/3)$, direct stationary covariance summation gives
\[
 H=a/2+2/3,\qquad
 \sigma^2=\tfrac7{12}a^2-\tfrac{13}{9}a+\tfrac{25}{27}.
\]
For the general Markov bound, subtract the two Berry--Esseen CDF bounds and
use that a standard normal density is at most $1/\sqrt{2\pi}$. Taking the
left CDF limit at the lower endpoint handles closed intervals and lattice
sources without an extra atom term.

\subsection{Published and Prefix Information Bands}

Extend a source trajectory beyond any hypothetical endpoint. For the published
midpoint rule, the sum of candidate hazards at positions with $I_t<B-k$ is
at most $2^{-k}/(1-\rho)$: read the positions in reverse order and use the
minimum information increment $-\log_2\rho$. A union bound gives the lower
tail in Eq.~\eqref{eq:published-band}. At the first crossing of $B$, the hazard
is one and the overshoot is less than $b$. This proves its upper tail and
almost-sure termination.

For continuous arithmetic-prefix coding, the intervals at the first crossing
of $B+r$ form an antichain partition of $[0,1)$, up to null boundaries. Under
Eq.~\eqref{eq:bounded-increments} every path reaches this crossing in finite
time. All intervals have width at most $2^{-B-r}$. Every unresolved interval
contains an interior payload-cell boundary. Disjoint intervals can be assigned
distinct boundaries, so their total probability is at most
$(2^B-1)2^{-B-r}<2^{-r}$. Previously resolved paths may be continued for this
argument; the bound is not conditioned on survival or successful delivery.
Since the crossing overshoots by less than $b$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:prefix-band}
follows. The same reasoning, with $r$ increasing, proves almost-sure prefix
resolution. At a fixed depth, split unresolved intervals by whether their
width exceeds $2^{-B-r}$; charging the remaining fine intervals to the same
boundaries proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:fixed-endpoint-failure}.

\subsection{Verified Midpoint Concentration}
\label{app:verified-proof}

Fix an original integer message $m\in\{0,\ldots,2^B-1\}$. In the uninterrupted
rotated process, write the selected interval as $[L_t,H_t)$, its width as
$W_t=2^{B-I_t}$, and its rotated point as $X_t$. Maintain a half-open interval
$[X_t,X_t+d_t)$ whose reverse replay maps each point by translation to
$[m,m+d_t)$. Initialize $d_0=1/2$ and update
\begin{equation}
 d_t=\min\{d_{t-1},H_t-X_t\}.
 \label{eq:safe-width}
\end{equation}
The old safe width avoids all earlier reverse-map cuts. The new right margin
avoids the selected-interval boundary and the current parent rotation cut.
This proves the invariant inductively. The right endpoint must be excluded:
it can be an earlier modulo discontinuity inside the current interval.
All points in the reverse image round to $m$.

The sufficient event
\begin{equation}
 0\le (L_t+H_t)/2-X_t<d_{t-1}
 \label{eq:safe-success}
\end{equation}
therefore guarantees midpoint acceptance and reverse recovery. The midpoint
is interior to the selected interval, so its positive displacement also lies
below the new right margin. Since $d_{t-1}\le1/2$, the original half-unit test
holds. This sufficient event does not require $W_t\le1$. The algorithm can
also accept outside it, so avoiding it upper-bounds verified survival.

Put $U_t=(X_t-L_t)/W_t$. Fresh continuous rotation gives
\[
 \Pr(Y_t=i,U_t\in du\mid\text{past})=q_t(i)\,du.
\]
Consequently the uninterrupted tokens have law $Q$ and, conditional on a token
path, the $U_t$ are independent uniforms. This statement is used before
conditioning on stopping survival.

\paragraph{A two-step contraction}
Put $z=d_{t-1}/W_{t-1}$, $q=W_t/W_{t-1}$, and $V=1-U_t$.
The sufficient success event is
$V\in[1/2,\min\{1,1/2+z/q\})$. On failure,
$z'=d_t/W_t=\min\{z/q,V\}\le1/2$. For a nonnegative function $f$, the transition
with successful paths removed is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 (K_qf)(z)={}&\int_0^{1/2}f(\min\{z/q,v\})\,dv\\
              &+(1/2-z/q)_+f(z/q).
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:killed-kernel}
\end{equation}
Choose $f(z)=z^{-1/2}$. Integration gives
$K_qf(z)=\max\{\sqrt{q/z},\sqrt2\}$. For any nonnegative nonincreasing $g$,
$q\le\rho$ gives $K_qg\le K_\rho g$: increasing $z/q$ reduces the survival
region and increases the argument of $g$. Also $K_\rho f$ is nonincreasing.
Thus the following calculation dominates any two consecutive source
probabilities bounded by $\rho$:
\begin{equation}
 K_\rho^2f(z)=
 \begin{cases}
 \rho z^{-1/2},&z<\rho^2/2,\\
 \sqrt2(1+\rho/2-z/\rho),&\rho^2/2\le z<\rho/2,\\
 (1+\rho)/\sqrt2,&\rho/2\le z\le1/2.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
In all three cases,
\begin{equation}
 K_\rho^2f(z)\le\lambda f(z),\qquad\lambda=(1+\rho)/2<1.
 \label{eq:two-step-contraction}
\end{equation}
For the middle case, the maximum ratio to $f(z)$ is at most
$\sqrt{\rho(2+\rho)^3/27}\le(1+\rho)/2$, because
\[
 27(1+\rho)^2-4\rho(2+\rho)^3
 =(1-\rho)(4\rho^3+28\rho^2+49\rho+27)\ge0.
\]
The first and last cases follow directly. This is an analytic inequality;
the artifact's numerical quadrature is only a supplementary check.

\paragraph{Entering a small interval without selection bias}
Let $\kappa=\inf\{t:I_t\ge B+1\}$. Then $W_\kappa\le1/2$ and
$I_\kappa<B+1+b$. Iterating Eq.~\eqref{eq:safe-width} gives
\[
 z_\kappa=\frac{d_\kappa}{W_\kappa}
 =\min\left\{\frac1{2W_\kappa},
       \min_{1\le i\le\kappa}\frac{V_iW_i}{W_\kappa}\right\}.
\]
Conditional on the token path, for $0<u\le1$,
\[
 \Pr(z_\kappa<u\mid\text{tokens})
 \le u\sum_{i=1}^{\kappa}\frac{W_\kappa}{W_i}
 \le\frac{u}{1-\rho}.
\]
Here $1/(2W_\kappa)\ge1$ and $W_\kappa/W_i\le\rho^{\kappa-i}$. Since
$z_\kappa\le1$, integrating the tail gives
$\mathbb E[f(z_\kappa)]\le2/\sqrt{1-\rho}$. On verified survival through
$\kappa$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:safe-success} also failed, so $z_\kappa\le1/2$.
Keeping the survivor indicator and applying Eq.~\eqref{eq:two-step-contraction}
in pairs gives
\begin{align}
 \Pr(\tau_v>\kappa+2j)
 &\le\lambda^j\mathbb E[
    \mathbf1_{\{\tau_v>\kappa\}}f(z_\kappa)]\notag\\
 &\le\frac{2\lambda^j}{\sqrt{1-\rho}}.
 \label{eq:verified-survival}
\end{align}
No division by a survival probability is made. Equation~\eqref{eq:verified-survival}
proves almost-sure termination and, using the per-token bound $b$, the upper
tail at $B+1+b+2jb$. Verification only vetoes candidate stops, so the early
hazard union bound still bounds its lower tail. Adding the two tails proves
Lemma~\ref{lem:verified-band}. For Eq.~\eqref{eq:dependent-source}, the miss
bound is $4\,2^{-k}+4(7/8)^j$ for the band $[B-k,B+3+4j]$.

\subsection{Verified Zero-Varentropy Control}
\label{app:verified-zero}

For the three-state half-probability control, $W_j=2^{B-j}$ is deterministic.
At a candidate depth $t$, let $Z_j$ be the final midpoint reversed through
steps $t,\ldots,j+1$, and let $e_j=Z_j-X_j$. Initially
$e_t=W_t(1/2-U_t)$. Subtract the forward rotation from the inverse modulo
formula to obtain
\begin{equation}
 e_{j-1}=W_{j-1}\left[
 \left(U_{j-1}+\frac{e_j}{W_{j-1}}\right)\bmod1-U_{j-1}\right],
 \label{eq:reverse-error}
\end{equation}
where $U_0=m/2^B$. Rotation offsets, absolute interval locations, and selected
child indices cancel. The midpoint condition depends only on $e_t$, and
reverse verification accepts precisely when $-1/2<e_0\le1/2$. Thus every stop
decision is a function of $m$ and the normalized-position tape, independent of
the token path. Almost-sure termination follows from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:verified-survival} with $\rho=1/2$. The stopped law factors as
$\Pr(\tau_v=t)Q_t$, proving exact TV zero. The argument also covers a fixed
number of equiprobable children at every history; it is not a general
zero-varentropy theorem or a finite-grid claim.

\subsection{Public Endpoints under Approximation}

For any independent natural length law $\gamma$, the same sign argument as
Eq.~\eqref{eq:endpoint-identity} gives
$\operatorname{TV}(S,\gamma(n)Q_n)=1-\sum_n\gamma(n)\ell(n)$.
If another normalized law $P$ satisfies $\operatorname{TV}(P,S)\le\varepsilon$,
its length marginal $\ell_P$ differs from $\ell$ by at most $\varepsilon$.
Consequently its own-length comparator $C_P=\ell_P(n)Q_n$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{TV}(P,C_P)\ge1-\sum_n\ell(n)^2-2\varepsilon.
 \label{eq:endpoint-approximation}
\end{equation}
The reference $S$ must obey the stated public-stopping hypotheses on the same
normalized transcript space. A finite integer codec's reference must use its
actual integer predicate; its coding bound does not imply closeness to the
separate continuous prefix construction. Removing failures or conditioning on
delivery does not preserve an unconditional $\varepsilon$ guarantee.

There is also a packet-independent support statement. If an arbitrary delivered
law $P$ always emits exactly at the public first-resolution endpoint, then
$P(E)=1$, regardless of the packet distribution or retry policy. Against its
independent comparator, $C_P(E)=\sum_n\ell_P(n)\ell(n)$, yielding the lower
bound $1-C_P(E)$ without a packet-uniformity assumption. It is not an exact TV
identity for arbitrary $P$. The empirical endpoint replay estimates this event
on its explicitly selected delivery and natural-control populations.

\subsection{Finite Rotation Grids}
\label{app:rotation-grid}

This section concerns the unverified controlled adapter with a quantized source,
not the full-vocabulary upstream study. Suppose each rotation is independently
uniform on $M=2^k$ grid points and $F\mid M$.
For any parent interval of width $W$ and any prior point, rotation produces a
translated circular lattice with spacing $W/M$. A token interval of length
$Wq_i=Wf_i/F$ contains exactly $Mq_i$ lattice points, including boundary cases.
Thus the active token law is exactly $Q$ without assuming a uniform message.

Conditional on that token, the accepted half-unit window is a half-open
interval of length $\min(Wq_i,1)$. Its lattice-point count differs from its
length divided by lattice spacing by less than one. If $\widehat h_i$ is the
finite-grid hazard and $h_i$ the ideal hazard, then
\[
 |\widehat h_i-h_i|\le\frac{1}{Mq_i},\qquad
 \operatorname{TV}(\widehat K,K)
 =\sum_iq_i|\widehat h_i-h_i|\le\frac{N}{M}.
\]
The bound is uniform in the prior point, so conditioning on earlier survival
and then averaging does not invalidate it. Coupling these public token/stop
kernels for at most $T$ steps gives TV at most $\min(1,TN_{\max}/M)$.
Deterministic source failures at matched prefixes couple identically.

For $k=192$, $N_{\max}=64$, and the largest experimental cap $T=1024$, this
finite-grid bound is $2^{-176}$ under independent uniform draws. The artifact
checks a nonzero discrepancy exactly: $M=8$, $F=4$, $B=2$, and $T=3$ give TV
$1/96$ for each fixed message, both with and without a source failure at active
prefix $AA$. This bridge does not cover private rotations, reverse-recovery
flags, timing, verification-dependent stopping, or successful-run conditioning.
It also does not turn the experimental \texttt{random.Random} generator into a
cryptographic primitive.
