\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

We evaluate four distinct questions: whether finite-input diagnostics describe
the executed sampling law, whether stopping creates a signal after length
matching, whether the guarded codec repairs its stated defect, and how these
mechanisms appear in a real text-channel implementation. Exact enumeration,
synthetic simulation, archived-result audit, and fresh model experiments are
kept separate. We do not turn a failed classifier or a successful decode into
an indistinguishability claim.

\subsection{Exact Coding and Stopping Audits}

The original NLS result and the RRC stopped-law example in
Sections~\ref{sec:audit} and~\ref{sec:stopping} are exhaustively reproducible.
The former executes pinned original code on all 256 one-byte inputs. The latter
uses exact rational probabilities, including natural strings absent from the
stego support. Neither result is a Monte Carlo estimate.

For Ghostext, we enumerate the complete capped transcript tree for an eight-bit
uniform payload and guard precisions $g\in\{0,2,4,8,16,32,64,128\}$, with a
32-step horizon. We use three sources: uniform over 64 tokens; a three-token
source proportional to $(0.61,0.28,0.11)$ after quantization; and a
context-dependent source alternating between that table and a four-token table
according to the observed prefix. The reference follows the same public prefix
stopping rule. Zero-width reference branches and horizon exhaustion remain in
the probability space.

Table~\ref{tab:guard-tv} shows the intended effect of retaining random suffix
precision. The exact values obey Theorem~\ref{thm:guard}; the enumerator computes
them from path probabilities rather than substituting that bound. The uniform
case already reaches zero at $g=4$, because its dyadic partitions align with
payload blocks. Nonuniform tables retain finite rounding error. These are
coding-law measurements under a uniform source, not measurements of ciphertext
pseudorandomness or of ordinary natural-language security.

Additional proof-support checks verify 19,200 safe-interval interior points
and 98,304 exact reverse-error identities. They detect replacing a half-open
safe interval by a closed one. Six complete prefix-tree enumerations check
Eq.~\eqref{eq:endpoint-identity} at a public cap of eight tokens, retaining
unresolved cap outcomes, and fixed-depth enumerations check
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fixed-endpoint-failure}. These finite checks supplement the
proofs; they do not establish an asymptotic claim by simulation.
The separate finite verified-rule certificate uses rational kernel integration
and downward-rounded integer masses, with all omitted mass retained as an
error budget. Its 512/2048-bit bounds in Appendix~\ref{app:finite-certificate}
are rigorous finite calculations rather than Monte Carlo estimates.

\subsection{Stopping with an Identical Length Histogram}

We simulate the published ideal RRC stopping law for the fixed binary source
$p=0.75$, using 20,000 outputs per class at each $B$. The natural control receives
a permutation of the stego lengths and independent fresh content, so the two
empirical length histograms are exactly equal. We fix the detector score before
sampling to $-|I(y)-B|$; there is no trained text model or fitted threshold.
We also measure the fixed event
$B-8\le I(y)\le B+2$, whose stopped-law probability is at least $0.984375$ by
the hazard bound. The AUC rises from $0.666$ at eight bits to $0.969$ at 2048 bits; at 2048 bits the stopped and control band rates are $0.994$ and $0.082$. Complete outputs and seeds are retained.



The growing gap is consistent with Theorem~\ref{thm:stopping}, but the theorem
is established by its proof rather than these simulations. The uniform binary
source is a negative control: its stopping time is independent of token
identities, giving exact equality with the independently length-matched law.

An independent dynamic program propagates surviving and stopped probability
mass over length and rare-symbol count. It agrees with the simulation's band
probabilities and retains total mass to within $2\cdot10^{-12}$. At $B=2048$,
the count-and-length TV is $0.915$ for $p=0.75$, whereas it is zero, up to
numerical precision, for the uniform source at every tested length. This
observable projection gives a lower bound on full ordered-transcript TV.
Figure~\ref{fig:finite-effects} separates the decreasing coding error from the
increasing stopped-text signal.

\subsection{Current Upstream Code and an Endpoint Control}
\label{sec:upstream-study}

We separately execute RRC commit \texttt{dae3262} (September 2026)~\cite{rrc2026code}, including its
unchanged \texttt{embed\_message} and \texttt{extract\_message} functions. This
study uses the upstream full GPT-2 vocabulary and Decimal-converted CDFs;
there is no Ghostext truncation, frequency quantization, or stability filter.
The public information score uses differences of the actual cumulative
boundaries, which need not equal the separately reported softmax values.

A frozen hash ordering of the released C4 prompt rows chooses 20 distinct
clusters: ten development and ten test prompts, with four tapes at each of
128 and 512 bits, totaling 160 secret instances. The unverified and verified
arms share a secret and rotation tape. A separate fixed-endpoint control uses
independent secrets and rotations, stopping at the verified arm's length,
which is independent of its own content. A fresh natural stream is sampled
from the same boundary-defined source using lazy unbiased-bit comparisons;
its prefixes provide exactly paired token lengths. Experimental random tapes
remain seeded PRNG streams. Each complete output receives a fresh upstream
receiver replay. The context window is 900 tokens and the cap is $2B$.

The fixed-endpoint arm is a separately written control using upstream active
primitives, not an unchanged encoder. An excluded four-case harness pilot
found one precision-exhaustion failure when this control continued past
payload completion. Before confirmatory generation, its Decimal reserve was
increased as a fixed function of the prescribed endpoint and vocabulary size;
the stopping arms retained the upstream 40-digit margin. Both pilot versions,
including that failure, are retained. No prompts, scores, or sample counts
were adjusted using confirmatory outcomes.

All 160 instances complete in all three arms and their natural controls, with
no context trimming. On token IDs, the unverified receiver recovers 159/160
messages; verification vetoes one candidate stop, adds one token, and gives
160/160 recovery. Upstream serialization decodes the prompt plus continuation
while skipping special tokens. Only 130 generated texts per arm and 132
natural texts preserve their tokenization. This leaves 110 eligible matched
pairs for either stopping arm and 107 for the independent-endpoint control.
Visible-text recovery is 129/160 unverified and 130/160 verified. These losses
are retained, not attributed to the stopping theorem or silently filtered from
reliability counts.

\input{tables/upstream-results}

The prespecified score remains $-|I(y)-B|$, with the higher development-control
95th percentile and a strict-greater decision. Both stopping arms have held-out
AUCs $0.938/0.998$ at 128/512 bits (Table~\ref{tab:upstream-results}). The eligible
slices contain ten and nine test prompt clusters, respectively. At 128 bits,
the observed FPR is $0.200$; the zero FPR at 512 bits is only $0/21$ controls.
Neither result establishes a calibrated low-FPR deployment operating point.
Requiring successful text recovery changes unverified 128-bit AUC to $0.934$
and leaves the verified results unchanged. A separate raw-token sensitivity
retains all 160 instances, including serialization failures: verified AUCs are
$0.926/0.996$, versus $0.542/0.534$ for the fixed-endpoint control. This is a
raw-token comparison, not a visible-text result.

The fixed-endpoint AUCs are $0.533/0.586$, with intervals that include $0.5$.
These are a pipeline control, not empirical proof of equality. Its delivery
cost is substantial at these independently chosen budgets: only 74/160
messages recover from IDs and 61/160 from rendered text. The primary control
analysis keeps these failures. Figure~\ref{fig:upstream-endpoints} shows the
information-gap distributions. The ideal fixed-endpoint sampling argument,
not a nonsignificant test, explains why decoupling the endpoint removes the
particular stopping mechanism. Larger independently chosen budgets can improve
recovery at a rate cost.

A public-feature replay selects the first eligible held-out case per bit length
and arm. Scoring uses only visible text, public prompt, model, and $B$, with no
secret or rotation tape. All twelve arm/control records reproduce their saved
token IDs and every selected CDF boundary; identical inputs shared across arms
reduce this to nine distinct model replays. The upstream empirical result
therefore concerns the current numerical implementation separately from the
continuous verified-rule theorem.

\subsection{Supporting Studies under Processed Sources}

Earlier controlled studies use GPT-2 and the Qwen3.5-2B GGUF backend with
Ghostext's processed candidate source and a separate rational-arithmetic RRC
adapter. Ten topic families are split into five development and five test
families before generation; Qwen has paired English/Chinese prompts. Each
context has eight tapes at 128 and 512 bits. The same public information score,
development-quantile threshold, and topic-cluster intervals are used. These
studies support the mechanism under different sources; they are not additional
runs of the unchanged upstream implementation.

The strict-source cohorts retain all 160 GPT-2 and 320 Qwen planned pairs.
Candidate-stability failures leave 121/123 completed GPT-2 stego/control
outputs and 301/315 Qwen outputs, with only 90/296 complete canonical matched
pairs. Their held-out AUCs at 128/512 bits are $0.865/0.934$ for GPT-2 and
$0.950/0.973$ for Qwen. Three and sixteen completed stego outputs fail the
sender-side reverse check. All counts and the recovery-conditioned sensitivity
are retained; these conditional AUCs do not describe every attempted output.

A separate source-expansion follow-up was motivated by two failed development
cases. Only when the initial truncated pool has no stable candidate, it searches
the top 4096 ranked candidates and retains at most 64 stable entries. Both
classes use the changed source, identified by a distinct protocol and seed.
All 160/320 new pairs complete canonically with matched lengths. The AUCs are
$0.862/0.986$ and $0.955/0.965$, with four/eight reverse-recovery failures.
This is a post-hoc source-robustness result, not a distribution-preserving
repair or an estimate for ordinary EOS generation. Independent public replays
on twelve strict-source and twelve expanded-source texts reproduce the saved
token IDs and every selected probability. Full settings, failure traces,
threshold results, and intervals remain in the artifact.

\subsection{Authenticated Delivery and Coding Repair}

Fresh authenticated trials use the same bilingual prompt families, two
repetitions, guards $g=0$ or $128$, and at most ten attempts per trial.
A 16-byte plaintext produces a 104-byte packet; natural tails are disabled.
Every completed trial retains its failed prefixes. This subsection's Qwen
model is the Qwen3.5-2B GGUF backend.

The Qwen process stopped after 64 saved trials and resumed from its original
source profile; those saved rows remain byte-identical. The interrupted
in-flight task had no saved result and received a fresh budget. We exclude
\texttt{01\_zh\_corner\_cafe:r0:g128} from primary bounded-attempt, rate, and
probe estimates, leaving 40 legacy and 39 guarded Qwen trials. The resumed
record succeeds, but does not establish its original outcome: the original
40-case guarded plan has conservative bounds of 38--39 ten-attempt deliveries
and 36--37 first-attempt deliveries. Unknown interrupted work is excluded
from timing.

Table~\ref{tab:repair-matrix} reports delivery, attempts, and net plaintext
rate. Every exhausted trial fails at the strict candidate filter, which the
coding repair does not change. The retained Qwen retries also include five
low-entropy restarts. Gross packet rates around $2.7$--$3.6$ bits/token are
substantially higher than the reported net rates because framing adds 88 bytes.

The legacy bootstrap-admissibility probe accepts all nine eligible GPT-2
stego prefixes versus four controls, and all 37 Qwen stego prefixes versus
nineteen controls. With guard 128, it accepts both classes on all thirteen
GPT-2 and 37 Qwen matched pairs. This removes that measured zero-width
rejection signal; it does not prove packet uniformity or complete-text
security. Twelve independent public replays reproduce the cached bootstrap
outcomes and stopping positions. The next test uses all fifty previously
eligible guarded pairs without a new outcome-dependent inclusion rule.

The artifact also preserves an earlier 100-delivery archive and an 18-pair
bootstrap pilot. The latter used filtered floating probabilities with three
unfiltered-argmax fallbacks, so neither historical cohort is pooled with the
strict-source or unchanged-upstream comparisons.

\subsection{The Endpoint Signal after Coding Repair}
\label{sec:endpoint-replay}

We specify this additional replay after the delivery study and the endpoint
analysis, making it a post-hoc diagnostic. It uses every previously eligible
guard-128 pair: thirteen GPT-2 and 37 Qwen pairs, with the same interrupted-case
exclusion. The observer additionally knows the 16-byte plaintext length,
giving public segment sizes of 60 and 44 bytes. The classifier takes only
visible text, prompt, and public source parameters. It retokenizes, advances
the two integer intervals, and accepts precisely when the second segment first resolves
at the visible endpoint. It does not use a key or decrypt a header, and checks
prefix resolution rather than all encoder abort predicates.

\input{tables/endpoint-replay}

All fifty delivered texts satisfy the endpoint condition
(Table~\ref{tab:endpoint-replay}). No GPT-2 control does; one Qwen control does.
Among the other controls, eight GPT-2 and nineteen Qwen texts resolve earlier,
while five and seventeen remain unresolved. Thus the guarded bootstrap probe's
acceptance of both classes does not remove this separate endpoint signal.
True-positive acceptance is structural for correctly replayed no-tail
deliveries; it is not learned generalization. The observed FPRs are conditional
on the original complete-control slice. Nominal 95\% Wilson intervals are
$[0,0.228]$ for GPT-2 and $[0.005,0.138]$ for Qwen, ignoring within-family
dependence. The small cohort does not establish a deployment-wide low FPR.

An independent integer-only analysis reproduces every decision from saved CDF
boundaries. After classification, all fifty reconstructed packet hashes match
the original successful attempts, and all 13,813 inspected natural selected
masses match their archived values. An initial single-thread GPT-2 replay kept
token IDs but changed some frequencies and failed packet fidelity; its complete
records are retained solely as a runtime-mismatch diagnostic. Final replays
use the original two-thread configuration. These checks support faithful public
replay, without claiming that every historical CDF boundary was saved or that
the packet distribution satisfies the ideal endpoint identity.

\paragraph{Earlier verification diagnostics}
A separate 40-case GPT-2 adapter ablation vetoed no stops and is only an
unchanged-path check. A targeted replay of all three completed reverse failures
from the original adapter cohort adds one, two, and two tokens; fresh text
receivers then recover each message. The exact finite-tape audit also exercises
a veto and budget exhaustion. These diagnostics concern the adapter;
Section~\ref{sec:upstream-study} provides the separate unchanged-upstream result.
