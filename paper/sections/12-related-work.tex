\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-positioning}

\paragraph{Security definitions and coding.}
Classical steganographic security compares cover and stego distributions or
uses computational distinguishing games~\cite{cachin1998information,hopper2002provably}.
Distribution-aware coding predates neural language models, including P-code
constructions~\cite{le2003pcodes} and model-based
steganography~\cite{sallee2004model,cox2005information}. Our guarded-prefix bound
is specific to the implemented codec and stopping rule; random continuation
and inverse-width rounding analysis are not presented as new general ideas.
Information random walks and phrase-length stopping have a substantial
source-coding literature~\cite{drmota2010tunstall}. We use elementary
anticoncentration to establish a security separation between two precisely
specified stopped-text laws.

\paragraph{Neural and cryptographic linguistic steganography.}
NLS connects arithmetic coding to neural generation~\cite{ziegler2019neural};
self-adjusting and grouping approaches explore security and capacity
tradeoffs~\cite{dai2019towards,shen2020near,zhang2021provably}.
Meteor~\cite{kaptchuk2021language}, Discop~\cite{ding2023discop}, and minimum
entropy coupling~\cite{dewitt2024perfect} develop stronger randomized
constructions. Discop explicitly discusses finite-message probability
granularity and padding. Our NLS experiment instead identifies a concrete
mismatch between an executed finite-input encoder and its own reported KL.
Meteor's fresh masking and Discop's randomized fixed-length experimental path
prevent transferring that specific counterexample indiscriminately.

RRC~\cite{yan2026rrc} recognizes small-interval distortion and replaces the
sampling point through fresh rotation. Our analysis agrees with its active
next-token argument and examines the separate stopping step. The exact
counterexample and asymptotic theorem concern its published midpoint rule;
later code that verifies extraction before stopping is a distinct algorithm.
This distinction is necessary both for fair comparison and for reproducibility.

\paragraph{Operational constraints and stopping.}
OD-Stega~\cite{huang2026odstega} optimizes the distribution--capacity tradeoff and
addresses tokenization ambiguity; it already assumes pre-encrypted input bits.
Yan and Murawaki~\cite{yan2025tokenization} give the stepwise retokenization
verification used here. Accordingly, neither encryption-first processing nor
candidate-level verification is claimed as Ghostext's novelty. Johnston's
study~\cite{johnston2026entropy} discusses payload-completion stopping, visible
length leakage, and EOS-based segmentation. Our stopping separation remains
after matching the full marginal length distribution and concerns residual
content--length dependence.

\paragraph{Empirical detection.}
A successful trained detector establishes a practical signal for its evaluation
setting; a failed detector does not prove indistinguishability. We therefore
combine explicit events and exact transcript laws with real-model measurements.
General generated-text detection methods such as DetectGPT~\cite{mitchell2023detectgpt}
address a different labeling problem from stego-versus-nonstego text generated
by the same model. Our controls share the model and candidate source so that
ordinary model-identification signals are not the intended explanation.
