\section{Ethical Considerations}
\label{sec:ethical-considerations}

This work studies a dual-use communication mechanism. Concealment can support
privacy, but can also hide harmful coordination. Our experiments use public
models, fixed experimental prompts, and synthetic test messages on local machines.
They do not target deployed third-party services or involve human participants,
private communications, or attempts to bypass a live moderation system.

A principal risk is giving users unwarranted confidence in a covert channel.
We therefore report detectable structure, failure cases, and the exact scope
of security bounds, and do not describe the prototype as safe for at-risk
communication. Test credentials and seeds are intentionally public and are
not operational secrets.

The external counterexamples concern published algorithms and locally executed
public code. Before submission, the authors should provide the relevant
maintainers with pinned reproductions, distinguish historical algorithms from
later fixes, and record their responses or a disclosure plan. This working
draft does not claim that such notifications have already been sent. No live
system exploit or personal data is needed to reproduce the findings.
