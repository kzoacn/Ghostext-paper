\section{Security of Complete Stopped Texts}
\label{sec:stopping}

\subsection{Independent Length Matching and Information Bands}

Fix a public token source $Q$, including its initial context, and write
$I_n(y)=-\log_2 Q(y_{1:n})$. For a stopped encoder law $P_B$, let
$\ell_B(n)=P_B(\tau=n)$. Our comparator draws this exact marginal length
independently of a fresh source trajectory:
\begin{equation}
 C_B(y_{1:n},n)=\ell_B(n)Q(y_{1:n}).
 \label{eq:independent-length}
\end{equation}
Length alone has no distinguishing power in this comparison. Dependence between
the content and its endpoint remains observable. Sampling from $Q$ with the
encoder's own content-dependent stopping rule defines a different reference.

The following event bound states the common mechanism. Its probability tools
are elementary; the substantive premises are proved separately for each encoder.

\begin{theorem}[Terminal-information criterion]
\label{thm:criterion}
Suppose supported token probabilities are at least $2^{-b}$, $B>k\ge0$, and
$P_B\{B-k\le I_\tau\le B+r\}\ge1-\delta$, for $r\ge0$. Put
$n_0=\lceil(B-k)/b\rceil$. Then
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{TV}(P_B,C_B)\ge1-\delta-
 \sup_{n\ge n_0,x}Q\{I_n\in[x,x+k+r]\}.
 \label{eq:band-criterion}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Proof}
Use the terminal-information band as the distinguishing event. Since
$I_n\le nb$, this event is impossible under $Q$ when $n<n_0$. For every other
length its probability is at most the displayed supremum. Average over the
independent length draw and subtract from the encoder's event probability.
No early-length approximation or successful-delivery conditioning is needed.

For a fixed finite-state irreducible aperiodic Markov source with positive
varentropy rate $\sigma^2$, a classical Berry--Esseen theorem gives
\begin{equation}
 \sup_x Q\{I_n\in[x,x+w]\}
 \le\frac{w/(\sigma\sqrt{2\pi})+2A_Q}{\sqrt n},
 \label{eq:markov-anticoncentration}
\end{equation}
where $A_Q<\infty$ depends on the source and initial state. This follows from
Kontoyiannis and Verd\'u's Theorem~21, including nonstationary initialization
in their Section~VI~\cite{kontoyiannis2014lossless}. Their general theorem does
not supply a numerical $A_Q$. We also give an elementary explicit bound for a
dependent example below. A terminal band of width $o(\sqrt B)$ with vanishing
miss probability therefore forces TV to one under these conditions.

\subsection{A Public Endpoint Can Be an Exact Test}
\label{sec:public-endpoint}

Some stopping predicates are public even though the encoded value is secret.
For arithmetic-prefix coding, an observer can test whether the current interval
lies inside \emph{some} payload cell without knowing which cell. Let $\tau$
be an almost surely finite deterministic public stopping time for $Q$, and
let $S(y,n)=Q(y)\mathbf1\{\tau(y)=n\}$, with length law $\ell$.
Then the event $E=\{\tau(y)=|y|\}$ attains
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{TV}(S,\ell(n)Q_n)
 =1-\sum_n\ell(n)^2.
 \label{eq:endpoint-identity}
\end{equation}
Indeed, $S\ge\ell(n)Q_n$ on $E$ and $S=0$ elsewhere. The test has TPR one and
FPR $\sum_n\ell(n)^2$. This is a stopping-time corollary of the standard
TV dependence calculation associated with the Gini--Simpson
index~\cite{perrone2024entropy}, not a new probability identity. The protocol
consequence is that accurate sampling up to a public decoding endpoint can
still be separated exactly from independent length matching.

The test must recognize the \emph{first} endpoint exactly at the end, rather
than merely successful resolution by then. The latter event has FPR
$(1+\sum_n\ell(n)^2)/2$. A public cap can be included as an endpoint if all cap
outcomes are retained. Contexts must be conditioned on separately: with a
public prompt $H$, the collision term is averaged over $\ell_H$, not computed
from a pooled prompt histogram. Canonical text replay is required to transfer
a token-level predicate to the visible-text channel. Privately randomized
midpoint stopping is outside this exact identity; it is handled by
Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion}.

\subsection{Three Terminal-Information Bounds}
\label{sec:terminal-bands}

For the following ideal mechanisms assume
\begin{equation}
 2^{-b}\le q_t(Y_t\mid Y_{<t})\le\rho<1.
 \label{eq:bounded-increments}
\end{equation}
Thus every information increment lies between $-\log_2\rho>0$ and $b$.
The midpoint mechanisms use exact real arithmetic and fresh independent
continuous rotations; the prefix mechanism uses a uniform continuous target.
These assumptions do not describe finite-precision model software automatically.

\paragraph{Published midpoint rule}
RRC's published Algorithm~3 stops when the rotated point lies within a half-unit
of the selected interval's midpoint~\cite{yan2026rrc}. After $t$ tokens the width
is $W_t=2^{B-I_t}$. Conditional on the selected token, the rotated point is
uniform in that interval, giving hazard $h_t=\min\{1,2^{I_t-B}\}$ and stopped law
\begin{equation}
 P_B(y_{1:t})=Q(y_{1:t})\prod_{i<t}(1-h_i)h_t.
 \label{eq:stopped-law}
\end{equation}
The extra factor is public given $Q$ and $B$. For example, with $B=1$ and
$Q(A)=3/4$, the complete outputs $A,B,AA,AB,AAA,AAB$ have probabilities
$1/2,1/4,1/6,1/16,1/64,1/192$. Exact enumeration gives TV $33/256$ against the
independent control with the same entire length marginal. This addresses the
published Appendix~B.3 stopping argument without challenging active next-token
preservation. It is not the exact law of the later verified implementation.

For any $k\ge0$, a reverse geometric sum of early hazards gives
\begin{equation}
 P_B(I_\tau<B-k)\le\frac{2^{-k}}{1-\rho},\qquad I_\tau<B+b.
 \label{eq:published-band}
\end{equation}
The upper bound holds because the first crossing of $B$ stops certainly.
Neither argument requires independent token identities.

\paragraph{Midpoint rule with reverse verification}
The current implementation can veto a candidate endpoint when reverse replay
does not recover the original message. Its hazard is no longer $h_t$.
Nevertheless, verification does not remove concentration of the terminal
information in the corresponding continuous idealization.

\begin{lemma}[Verified midpoint band]
\label{lem:verified-band}
For any fixed $B$-bit message, or an independent message mixture, let $\tau_v$
be the ideal midpoint rule with reverse verification. For integers $j\ge0$
and $k\ge0$, with $\lambda=(1+\rho)/2$,
\begin{align}
 &P_B\{I_{\tau_v}\notin[B-k,B+1+b+2jb]\}\notag\\
 &\qquad\le\frac{2^{-k}}{1-\rho}
       +\frac{2\lambda^j}{\sqrt{1-\rho}}.
 \label{eq:verified-band}
\end{align}
The rule terminates almost surely.
\end{lemma}

The new upper-tail argument tracks a one-sided interval whose every point
reverses safely to the original message. Its normalized width need not be
bounded away from zero. A two-step contraction for the reciprocal square root
of that width bounds survival without conditioning away small safe intervals.
Appendix~\ref{app:verified-proof} gives the invariant and full calculation.
This proof concerns continuous rotations and exact reals. It does not transfer
the old hazard or a finite-grid theorem to verification-dependent software.

\paragraph{Arithmetic-prefix termination}
Let $U$ be uniform on $[0,1)$, its payload be $\lfloor2^B U\rfloor$, and its
remaining binary expansion supply independent random continuation. Generate
nested $Q$-arithmetic intervals containing $U$ and stop when one fits in a
payload cell. Containment requires $I_\tau\ge B$. At the first crossing
$\kappa$ of $B+r$, every interval has width at most $2^{-B-r}$. An unresolved
interval straddles an interior payload boundary; the disjoint intervals can
be charged to at most $2^B-1$ such boundaries. Hence
\begin{equation}
 P\{B\le I_\tau\le B+r+b\}\ge1-2^{-r}.
 \label{eq:prefix-band}
\end{equation}
This is an unconditional bound for the uniform payload mixture. It does not
assume that authenticated packet bytes are uniform or equate a finite guard
with infinite random continuation.

\subsection{Separation on Dependent Sources and Its Limits}

\begin{theorem}[Stopping separation]
\label{thm:stopping}
Fix a finite-state irreducible aperiodic Markov source satisfying
Eq.~\eqref{eq:bounded-increments} with positive varentropy rate. For each of
the three ideal mechanisms above, with its stated message assumptions, let
$C_B$ use that mechanism's own exact marginal stopping length independently
of fresh source content. Then
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{TV}(P_B,C_B)\ge
 1-O_Q\!\left(\frac{\log B}{\sqrt B}\right)\longrightarrow1.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

Choose $k,r,j$ of order $\log B$ in the respective band bounds and apply
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:band-criterion}--\eqref{eq:markov-anticoncentration}. For a fully
explicit dependent example, take public initial state zero and
\begin{equation}
 P=\begin{pmatrix}3/4&1/4\\1/2&1/2\end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:dependent-source}
\end{equation}
The row entropies are $0.811278$ and $1$ bits; stationary adjacent information
increments have covariance $0.0393683>0$. Thus this is not merely dependent
symbols with IID information increments. Conditioning on even states makes
odd bridges independent. A repeated bridge type supplies a scaled binomial
component, yielding, for $n\ge2$,
\begin{equation}
 \sup_x Q\{I_n\in[x,x+w]\}\le
 \min\!\left\{1,\frac{2K(\lfloor w/d\rfloor+1)}{\sqrt{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}}\right\},
 \label{eq:explicit-markov}
\end{equation}
where $d=\log_2(3/2)$ and $K=11\sqrt\pi/12$. The varentropy rate is
$0.4269101$ bits squared per token. Appendix~\ref{app:markov-proof} proves the
bound and positivity without a numerical limit approximation.

\paragraph{A finite certificate}
The closed-form envelopes above can be conservative. For this same chain,
started in state zero, exact integration of the safe-rule kernel and integer
probability bounds instead certify verified-rule TV at least $0.1347$ at
$B=512$ and $0.4294$ at $B=2048$. The retained 128-bit certificate is vacuous.
The calculation covers every natural length, including those beyond its finite
scan, without estimating the encoder's length law. Appendix~\ref{app:finite-certificate}
gives the recurrence, error accounting, and complete rational bounds. These
are finite results for the ideal continuous-rotation mechanism on the stated
dependent source, not predictions of model AUC or a transfer to Decimal software.

\paragraph{A dependent negative control}
Consider three states, each staying or advancing modulo three with probability
one half. The chain is irreducible and aperiodic, but every supported length-$n$
path has $I_n=n$, so its varentropy is zero. For all three ideal mechanisms,
TV against the independently length-matched source is exactly zero. Prefix
resolution occurs at depth $B$; unverified midpoint hazards depend only on
length. For the verified rule, deterministic widths make the reverse-error
recursion depend only on normalized rotation positions, which are independent
of the token path (Appendix~\ref{app:verified-zero}). This particular exception
shows why positive entropy alone is insufficient for the theorem; zero
varentropy alone is not asserted to secure every stopping mechanism.

\paragraph{A design consequence with a delivery cost}
A mechanism retaining a vanishing-error $o(\sqrt B)$ terminal-information window
cannot remove this separation on the stated sources. At a fixed exogenous
depth $N$, continuous arithmetic sampling instead has output law exactly $Q_N$.
Its prefix-recovery failure satisfies, for every $r\ge0$,
\begin{equation}
 P(\text{recovery fails at }N)
 \le Q\{I_N<B+r\}+2^{-r}.
 \label{eq:fixed-endpoint-failure}
\end{equation}
Split unresolved intervals into those wider than $2^{-B-r}$ and fine intervals
straddling payload boundaries; the same boundary charge proves the inequality.
The output-law statement keeps failures and assumes a uniform payload with
independent continuation. Conditioning on successful delivery changes it.
A sublinear extra information budget can leave the narrow-band regime, so this
is a termination constraint, not an impossibility theorem for rate-efficient
steganography. Neither fixed endpoints nor this ideal bound establishes
security for arbitrary encrypted packets, finite precision, or general LLMs.
