# Independent review of the S&P 2027 revision

Review date: 2026-10-20. Evidence inspected through approximately 15:45 UTC.

**Recommendation: weak accept. Confidence: moderate.**

I recommend weak accept for the paper as a narrowly scoped security analysis and implementation audit. The principal reason is the public stopping likelihood and the asymptotic separation after matching the entire length distribution. I do not recommend acceptance on the strength of a new secure steganographic system, a broad attack on current deployed RRC software, or a demonstrated low-false-positive detector. The paper does not establish those outcomes.

I inspected the included manuscript sections and proof appendix, implementation/specification, exact-audit scripts, stopping and delivery experiment code, retained results, and relevant primary literature. I did not read or inherit the previous review's recommendation. I ran lightweight exact computations and existing toy tests, but no language-model jobs. This report does not count incomplete Qwen cohorts or the newly started expanded-source cohorts as completed evidence.

The working manuscript and artifact changed during the review. Two concrete concerns I raised—the non-injective NLS stub renderer and the inaccurate characterization of historical natural controls—were corrected before this report. I inspected those corrections. The failed mutation test described below remains a required artifact correction at the review snapshot.

The most persuasive result is the RRC analysis. Under ideal independent uniform rotations, each active step samples the stated token distribution, but the probability of stopping after that token is `min(1, 1/W)`. Conditioning on the complete observed transcript therefore introduces the product of survival and stopping hazards. Fresh rotation removes the previous hidden point's bias; it does not remove this endpoint factor. I checked the conditional reasoning, the exact binary example, and the appendix's concentration and binomial anticoncentration bounds. I found no mathematical error. The assumption of a fixed nonuniform binary source is necessary, and the manuscript correctly identifies the uniform exception and the deterioration of constants near it.

This is a fair criticism of a specific published claim. Appendix B.3 of the [ACL 2026 RRC paper](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.39.pdf) argues from active token preservation to complete-text security and treats the stopping factor as inaccessible or harmless. The proposed factor is public under the manuscript's threat model. Matching the full stopping-length marginal is a stronger control than merely matching expected length. The result consequently supplies more than a length-only counterexample.

The distinction from later software is also essential. The [upstream changelog](https://github.com/ryehr/RRC_steganography/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md) describes a reverse-recovery check before accepting termination. The current paper explicitly restricts its exact law and theorem to the published midpoint rule. That restriction must remain in the abstract, conclusions, figures, and any publicity. The separately written verified adapter is not an execution or validation of the current upstream implementation.

The guarded coding theorem also appears correct under its stated assumptions. An unresolved interval contains an interior payload-block boundary; disjoint active intervals can therefore be charged to at most `2^B - 1` boundaries. Multiplying conditional rounding error by uniform-target interval mass cancels interval width, and sequential total-variation composition gives the stated bound. Absorbing invalid branches and horizon exhaustion are included, which avoids a common success-conditioning mistake. The resulting numerical bound is a coding-only statement for uniform segments with independent guards. It does not transfer automatically to the actual authenticated packet, successful retries, ordinary EOS generation, or a fixed chosen plaintext.

The novelty is concentrated rather than broad. [Discop](https://dingjinyang.github.io/uploads/Discop_sp23_paper.pdf) already discusses finite-message probability granularity, padding, and implementation distortion. Random continuation and interval-based coding have older antecedents. The manuscript appropriately avoids presenting these general ideas as new. Johnston's [2026 thesis](https://repositum.tuwien.at/bitstream/20.500.12708/228943/1/Johnston%20Emile%20-%202026%20-%20Entropy-Steering%20A%20Lookahead%20Approach%20to%20High-Capacity...pdf), especially Section 2.7, already discusses payload-completion endings, sentence finishing, length leakage, and EOS-based segmentation. I did not find the exact-marginal-length separation or this RRC hazard calculation there. The paper should continue to present that precise difference as its main contribution, with the guarded implementation as supporting engineering and analysis.

The NLS example is a useful executable counterexample to a diagnostic, rather than evidence of an attack rate against actual GPT-2 prose. I independently reproduced terminal TV `15/16` with reported KL zero using the pinned original encoder function. The initial stub rendered every two-token output as the same text, so its token discrepancy was not observable through that stub text channel. The revised stub uses injective Unicode rendering, checks retokenization, and enumerates all 4096 two-symbol natural strings; I reran it and obtained complete-text TV `15/16`, with 256 distinct stego texts. This closes the synthetic text-channel gap. Sentence completion is disabled and the model is synthetic; both conditions must stay prominent. [Meteor's fresh masking](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~kaptchuk/publications/ccs21_meteor.pdf) prevents this particular deterministic-lookahead-padding argument from being transferred directly. That does not by itself establish any statement about Meteor's complete stopping law.

**The main weaknesses and required checks are these.**

1. **The real-model stopping evidence is substantially selected.** The completed strict GPT-2 cohort contains 160 planned pairs, 121 completed stego outputs, 123 completed natural controls, and only 90 complete canonical matched pairs. There are 39 stego and 37 natural tokenization failures. Three completed stego outputs fail reverse recovery, with two such cases among matched pairs. At 128 and 512 bits, only 26 and 16 held-out matched pairs remain, respectively. I independently reproduced their AUCs, approximately 0.865 and 0.934. These support the stated conditional experiment, not an unconditional distribution of transmitted messages. The failures are correctly retained; merely retaining them does not remove selection. Put the denominators and failure categories next to the headline AUCs. The new source-expansion cohorts must remain separate because expanding the candidate search changes the source.

2. **There is no demonstrated low-FPR operating point.** The development threshold is fixed using development natural scores, but the strict GPT-2 held-out FPRs are approximately 0.115 and 0.063, with wide uncertainty. Only five held-out topic families are available, and several families contribute very few eligible examples. Cluster intervals are useful descriptive uncertainty for this design, not evidence of deployment-wide calibration. The current manuscript avoids a low-FPR claim; preserve that restraint. Known message length, exact model, exact prompt, and exact source processing are substantive assumptions, although reasonable for the stated counterexample and chosen-message setting.

3. **The historical Qwen pilot has a distinct reference law.** Inspection of `run_detector_block.py` showed sampling from floating-point `entry.probability`, not `frequency/F`, plus raw-argmax fallback when the stable set is empty. Three of the 18 natural texts record one fallback each, with positions not retained. All 18 natural texts are unique, and replayed visible lengths match their paired stego totals. The 11 zero-width rejections are real observations, but they do not isolate integer coding against exactly the same strict quantized source. The corrected evaluation now states this explicitly. Its 18 controls support a limited pilot, not a general detector claim.

4. **The verified real-model ablation does not exercise verification.** Across all 40 records in the completed GPT-2 verified-adapter cohort, `rejected_stop_count` is zero. Its 30 completed stego outputs therefore demonstrate no actual stop veto. The synthetic finite-tape fixture does exercise this branch. The real-model cohort cannot be used as evidence that the added check repaired a reverse-recovery failure, or as a security assessment of current upstream RRC. Targeted replay of observed reverse failures would be a useful diagnostic, but its selection must be explicit.

5. **The experimental stopping horizon needs explicit specification.** The real-model adapter caps generation at `2*B` tokens. Published Algorithm 3 and the ideal stopped law used by the theorem are uncapped; the one-bit exact example even includes length-three outcomes. The code retains a distinct `token_budget` outcome, but the cap should appear in the manuscript and machine-readable experimental parameters. No budget failures occur in the completed strict GPT-2 cohort I checked. This is a protocol-description gap, not a counterexample to the theorem.

6. **An existing regression test is invalid for the new codec and failed in my run.** `test_failures.py` ran ten tests; nine passed, while `test_mutated_text_fails` failed with “GhostextError not raised.” It assumes that replacing the last packet-bearing token must invalidate the packet. Guards intentionally make the text-to-payload mapping many-to-one: with uniform 64-way tables, `B=8,g=4`, token paths `[22,0]` and `[22,1]` both decode to byte `0x58`, which I checked independently. This is not an authentication forgery. The test should deterministically alter recovered packet bytes when it tests authentication failure, and separately test an allowed payload-preserving visible edit. The integrity discussion should mention that such edits can occur within the packet-bearing prefix as well as in ignored tails. A claimed clean regression result is blocked until this stale expectation is corrected.

7. **Freeze a reproducible artifact rather than relying on hashes alone.** Most historical source hashes I checked can be recovered from existing commits, but current source intentionally does not match earlier manifests, so old runs cannot be resumed from the current checkout. Provide a source-version/command map or immutable source snapshots for each cohort. Repair-matrix manifests currently omit several package versions recorded by stopping manifests, including Torch, Transformers, NumPy, and cryptography. Packet and guard randomness comes from the operating system, so delivery trials are statistically rerunnable and their saved texts replayable; they are not exactly regenerable from a task-order seed. An anonymous release, dependency environment, model acquisition hashes, and exact reproduction commands are still needed. These are final artifact obligations, not evidence that the saved measurements are fabricated.

I would not make the absence of ordinary EOS controls, unknown-prompt attacks, or unknown-length-mixture attacks a rejection condition for the current narrow claims. Those would answer different questions, and the manuscript says so. Conversely, the existing results cannot support a broader claim that the repaired prototype is secure or that all finite-message linguistic steganography is vulnerable in the same way. The improvement is an audit and a bounded local repair, with stopping and selection still explicitly outside the repair.

My independent checks reproduced the NLS and ideal RRC exact results, all 24 guarded exact-tree entries, the dynamic-program values at representative payload sizes including 2048 bits, and the strict GPT-2 matched-pair AUCs. The four guarded-codec tests and two bilingual toy round-trip tests passed. I checked cohort membership, unique identifiers, token/probability lengths, and visible-text hashes for the complete 160-pair GPT-2 stopping and 40-trial GPT-2 delivery cohorts. I found no numerical disagreement in those checks. The one regression-test failure is reported above rather than omitted.

The mathematical core merits publication if the paper remains centered on the public stopping factor, exact length matching, and the difference between coding fidelity and complete-text security. Its present practical scope and modest empirical sample size keep my recommendation at **weak accept**, not accept or strong accept. Incomplete new cohorts and promised future measurements play no role in that recommendation.
