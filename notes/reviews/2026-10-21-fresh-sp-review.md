**Summary and significance.** The paper shows that accurate active token sampling can coexist with a detectable relationship between a text's content and its endpoint. Its main comparison gives the natural sampler exactly the encoder's marginal output-length distribution, independently of the natural content. Narrow concentration of terminal source information then supplies a public distinguisher. The paper proves the requisite concentration for arithmetic-prefix recovery, published RRC stopping, and an idealized reverse-verified midpoint rule; combines this with Markov-source anti-concentration; supplies a dependent zero-varentropy exception; and describes the recovery cost of an independent endpoint. Separate exact audits expose the difference between a token-table diagnostic and an executed finite-input law. The current-code RRC experiment and authenticated Ghostext replay make the issue concrete.

The external target matters. RRC's published Appendix B.3 explicitly argues that its stopping factor does not enable efficient distinction. Ghostext's public hazard and terminal-information analysis address that argument directly. The independent length-matched comparator also removes an easy length-histogram objection. This is a security-relevant correction to a contemporary claim, with a useful general lesson about what object a steganographic security argument must cover. I checked the relevant algorithm and argument in the [published RRC paper](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.39.pdf).

**Novelty.** Finite-message probability granularity, random continuation, inverse-width rounding error, the need to specify a complete cover distribution, and ordinary stopping-time dependence are established ideas. The paper generally credits them correctly. The most convincing new technical component is the safe-interval argument and two-step contraction for reverse verification, followed by a common stopping separation for three specified mechanisms. The dependent-source example and exact executed-code audits add substance. The NLS example is valuable as a reproducible diagnostic counterexample, but it would be too small a contribution by itself. Likewise, the authenticated framing and guarded prototype are supporting engineering contributions. The distinction from Discop's earlier finite-message discussion is reasonable: that discussion establishes the underlying granularity concern, while this paper executes the original NLS function and analyzes the separate endpoint problem. I found no primary reference in the checks performed that already supplies the paper's reverse-verified concentration argument; this was not an exhaustive literature search.

**Correctness.** I found no concrete error that invalidates Theorems 1–3 or the supporting arguments in D.1–D.6. The terminal-information event in Theorem 1 is used correctly, including the lower bound on feasible lengths. The cited Markov Berry–Esseen result supports the anti-concentration step and permits nonstationary initialization; its unspecified source-dependent constant is appropriately acknowledged. I checked this against Section VI and Theorem 21 of [Kontoyiannis and Verdú](https://www.dpmms.cam.ac.uk/~ik355/PAPERS/SVYK-J.pdf). The bridge conditioning in D.1 gives independent odd states, and the common bridge type yields the stated binomial anti-concentration bound. The positive-varentropy calculation is consistent with that construction.

For the most delicate part, D.3, the half-open safe interval is essential. Its width retains a valid one-sided inverse image through earlier modulo cuts. The sufficient midpoint event gives correct reverse recovery; the killed kernel and its two-step contraction provide an upper bound on survival. The entry argument controls the inverse square root of the safe width before conditioning on survival, avoiding a selection gap at precisely the difficult step. The early-stop bound remains valid because verification only vetoes stops. I also checked that D.4's reverse-error recursion removes child locations for the particular equiprobable dependent source. These arguments establish the stated continuous, exact-arithmetic results. They do not establish the same theorem for current Decimal arithmetic, and the paper does not claim that transfer.

Theorem 3 also appears sound under its stated uniform-source assumption. At a fixed depth, unresolved integer intervals are disjoint and each crosses a payload-block boundary. Their probability weights cancel the local inverse-width error in the expected one-step bound. Sequential TV composition then applies on the normalized space that retains invalid and horizon outcomes. The stopping-matched reference and independent-length reference are kept distinct. The warning that authenticated encryption alone does not establish a uniform framed packet is necessary and well placed. The endpoint identity and the support-only statement for arbitrary delivered packets have appropriately different conclusions.

**Empirical design and claim scope.** The strongest empirical result is the unchanged upstream experiment: separate development/test prompt clusters, a fixed public score, common secrets and rotation tapes for verified/unverified arms, an independent secret and rotation tape for the endpoint control, and fresh receiver execution. Inspecting the pinned core and its wrapper supported the claim that the sender/receiver functions are unchanged and that instrumentation is observational. The boundary-defined natural source is a careful choice, since its masses need not equal separately reported softmax probabilities. Retaining serialization failures and receiver failures is also important. The fixed-endpoint control is useful evidence that the observed score is associated with stopping, and its poor recovery is reported rather than discarded.

There are material limits to that evidence:

1. The current-upstream study uses one model, two payload sizes, and twenty prompt clusters. Its principal visible-text analysis retains 110/160 pairs. At 512 bits, only 21 eligible test pairs across nine test prompts contribute to the headline AUC. The raw-token sensitivity is persuasive supporting evidence for the mechanism, but it does not recover an unconditional visible-text result. The endpoint control has a different canonical selection of 107 pairs, so its conditional comparison is a diagnostic control with a different selected population. The paper is explicit about this.
2. The 128-bit held-out FPR of 0.200 and the small 512-bit control denominator preclude a demonstrated low-FPR deployment attack. Cluster intervals acknowledge the dependence structure but cannot supply broad prompt or traffic generalization. Likewise, the authenticated endpoint result is a structural, post-hoc test on fifty selected no-tail deliveries, with known plaintext length.
3. The observer knows the source, prompt, and encoded length. The independent-length comparator is mathematically clean and appropriate to the questioned RRC claim, but ordinary conversation has its own content-dependent endpoints. Unknown-length mixtures, unknown prompts, natural tails, and ordinary EOS generation remain distinct security questions.
4. The asymptotic theorem is about fixed finite-state Markov sources with bounded supported probabilities and positive varentropy. The explicit lower bound is vacuous at the studied 128/512-bit sizes. The real-model evidence therefore supports the mechanism separately; it is not a numerical validation of the theorem's finite-size lower bound. Only one upstream candidate stop is vetoed, so the measured verified arm provides little empirical coverage of cases with frequent verification failures.

These limits reduce breadth and practical reach, but the current manuscript usually states them at the point where they matter. The earlier processed-source studies, source-expansion follow-up, interrupted delivery trial, and runtime-mismatch replay are separated clearly enough to avoid treating them as interchangeable evidence. The prototype's overhead, filtering failures, and numerical replay sensitivity constrain its operational value; the paper does not depend on it being a deployable secure channel.

**Blocking issues.** I did not identify a confirmed scientific or artifact blocker for the paper's stated contribution. In particular, I do not regard the small empirical cohorts as invalidating the exact counterexamples or the restricted theorem. The limitations above do prevent stronger claims about arbitrary LLMs, unconditioned transmitted-text populations, or low-FPR field detection. The recommendation depends on preserving the manuscript's present scope. I have not independently rerun the model cohorts, so I cannot certify their raw model-generation provenance beyond the retained records, code, hashes, and consistency checks.

**Optional improvements.** The main presentation would benefit from placing more emphasis on the stopped-transcript theorem and current upstream audit, while compressing the older prototype and cohort history. A compact table mapping each mechanism to its reference law, randomness assumptions, numerical realization, and established guarantee would make the paper substantially easier to review. A full visible-text analysis that accounts for all serialization outcomes, and a replication on another unchanged upstream model, would strengthen practical impact. An independent-endpoint budget curve showing recovery versus extra tokens would make the design guidance more actionable. Finally, make explicit that Meteor's fresh masking excludes the particular zero-padding counterexample; it should not be read as a blanket endorsement of every finite-output stopping policy. The later related-work wording already largely makes that distinction. None of these additions is required to repair a specific correctness flaw found in this review.
